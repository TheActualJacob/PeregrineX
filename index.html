The problem

Today, proof of safety is built by hand.

Scripted by intuition

Test engineers write the scenarios they can think of. The safety case becomes a list of guesses about which conditions matter. Coverage ends where intuition ends.

Failures are combinations

The dangerous failures are rarely single faults. They are combinations of individually manageable conditions: a gusting crosswind, a degraded sensor, an unexpected instruction. People do not enumerate that space. It is too large.

Proof is the bottleneck

A working prototype is not a certified aircraft. The distance between them is evidence, and building that evidence is the slowest step in the program.

How it works

From simulation to sign-off.

  1. 1

    Generate adversarial scenarios

    PeregrineX plugs into the flight simulation environment you already run. It generates rare but plausible scenarios designed to break the autonomy stack: combinations of weather, system failures, sensor degradation, traffic, and air traffic control behavior. Not the cases a test engineer would script. The ones between them.

  2. 2

    Search for the failure boundary

    ML-guided search concentrates sampling where behavior starts to degrade, then finds the exact conditions under which the stack makes an unsafe decision. This is falsification, not demonstration. Each failure is minimized to its root cause and made reproducible: same seed, same scenario, same outcome.

  3. 3

    Export certification evidence

    Every failure, and every clean sweep, is packaged as audit-ready certification evidence: versioned, seeded, and traceable to FAA requirements. Coverage an auditor can inspect. Runs an auditor can repeat. Evidence built for sign-off.
